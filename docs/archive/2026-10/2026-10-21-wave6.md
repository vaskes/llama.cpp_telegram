## "Claim vs implementation" lesson

Across six waves every reviewer who landed a "this is fine, looks
good" stamp on a tripwire had to walk it back the moment code that
the tripwire was supposed to catch actually changed. The two surviving
mutations above are case studies: each looks like a real failure until
you measure it, and neither is dangerous enough to remove the tripwire.

The lesson of this batch: do not call a tripwire "covered" on the
strength of a green test against current code. Mutate the code, watch
the tripwire go red, then put the code back. If the tripwire stays
green against the mutation, the tripwire is decoration and should be
either tightened or removed. The two inert-by-measurement mutations in
this batch both survived the audit because the tripwire text said one
thing and the assertion actually checked another — the audit's job was
to find them, and the audit found them.

A future change that lands and removes one of the two inert tripwires
is a regression in safety; a future change that lands and tightens one
of the two inert tripwires is a regression in maintenance. Neither
should happen without a fresh review.
